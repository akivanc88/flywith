The gap

A cheap fare doesn't tell you if the route is survivable with kids or older parents.

Google Flights shows the price. It won't tell you whether two terminal changes, a 2-hour sprint with a stroller, visa rules, and tired grandparents add up to a humane trip. FlyWith adds the missing decision layer — and often finds something cheaper anyway.

How it works

Not another generic flight search

FlyWith scores stopover cities by your family's real constraints — not just connection time.

1

Tell us your long-haul trip

Origin, destination, dates, and whether you're traveling with kids, parents, seniors, or a tight budget.

2

We score stopover value

Each option gets a worth-it score from fare, hotel estimate, visa friction, airport comfort, accessibility and family logistics.

3

You compare the tradeoff

See the standard route beside stopover options in plain language, including what still needs manual verification.

4

Book wherever's cheapest

FlyWith doesn't sell tickets. It explains the recommendation and hands off to Kiwi, Google Flights, Skyscanner or the airline.

Under the hood

Watch the agents decide

Every verdict comes from a team of AI agents: an orchestrator delegates to flight-search, value and logistics specialists in parallel — and a verifier audits every claim against the raw evidence before anything reaches you.

    Why FlyWith

    Families still do the hard part manually

    Competitors solve pieces of the journey. FlyWith focuses on the missing decision: whether a stopover is actually better for the people traveling.

    vs. Google Flights & Skyscanner

    Great inventory, no judgment

    Excellent flexible search — but you must already know how to model stopovers, multi-city fares, visas and separate-ticket risk yourself.

    vs. Airwander, Stovo, GetStopover

    Adventure, but not for families

    They frame stopovers as bonus destinations, yet rarely score family comfort, stroller logistics, senior accessibility or first-night recovery.

    vs. Going & Hopper

    Price alerts, not survivability

    Strong at deals and price confidence. They don't answer whether a cheaper route is actually doable with kids or older parents.

    vs. Airline stopover programs

    Perks with fine print

    Great when eligible, but rules vary by airline, route, fare, passport and timing. Travelers need a neutral comparison.

    Stopover confusion

    Travelers often learn through Reddit that 24+ hours is a stopover, not a layover, and that multi-city search may be required.

    Family travel pain

    Parents ask about stroller handling, layover length, play areas, sleep, snacks — and whether the cheapest route is worth the stress.

    Parents & seniors

    Adult children worry about wheelchair assistance, terminal walks, medication carry-on, rest windows and missed connections.
