How it works
Not another generic flight search
FlyWith scores stopover cities based on your family constraints, not just connection time.
1

Tell us your long-haul trip

Origin, destination, dates, passenger mix, and whether you are traveling with kids, parents, seniors, or a tight budget.

2

We score stopover value

Each option gets a worth-it score using fare, hotel estimate, visa friction, airport comfort, accessibility, and family logistics.

3

You compare the tradeoff

See the standard route beside stopover options, including what still needs manual verification before booking.

4

Book somewhere else

FlyWith does not sell tickets in v1. It explains the recommendation and hands off to Kiwi, Google Flights, Skyscanner, or an airline page.

    Market gap
    Families still do the hard part manually
    Competitors solve pieces of the journey. FlyWith focuses on the missing decision: whether a stopover is actually better for the people traveling.

    Google Flights and Skyscanner

    Excellent inventory and flexible search, but users must already know how to model stopovers, multi-city fares, visas, and separate-ticket risk.

    Airwander, Stovo, GetStopover

    They frame stopovers as bonus destinations, but rarely score family comfort, stroller logistics, senior accessibility, or first-night recovery.

    Going and Hopper

    Strong at alerts and price confidence. They do not answer whether a cheaper route is survivable with kids or older parents.

    Airline stopover programs

    Great perks when eligible, but rules vary by airline, route, fare, passport, and timing. Travelers need neutral comparison.

    Stopover confusion

    Travelers often discover through Reddit that 24+ hours is a stopover, not a layover, and that multi-city search may be required.

    Family travel pain

    Parents ask about stroller handling, layover length, play areas, sleep, snacks, and whether the cheapest route is worth the stress.

    Parents and seniors

    Adult children worry about wheelchair assistance, terminal walks, medication carry-on, rest windows, and missed connections.
